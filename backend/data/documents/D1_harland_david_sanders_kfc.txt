Title: Harland David Sanders and Kentucky Fried Chicken (KFC)
Subject: Entrepreneurship, Culinary History, and Franchise Innovation
Key Figures: Colonel Harland David Sanders, Pete Harman

Colonel Harland David Sanders was an American entrepreneur best known for founding the fast food chicken restaurant chain Kentucky Fried Chicken (now known globally as KFC). Born on September 9, 1890, in Henryville, Indiana, Sanders experienced hardship from an early age following the untimely death of his father. Throughout his youth and early adulthood, he worked a wide variety of occupations, including farmhand, railway fireman, blacksmith's helper, insurance salesman, and ferryboat operator.

In 1930, during the height of the Great Depression, Sanders took over a Shell service station on US Route 25 just outside Corbin, Kentucky. He noticed that travelers frequently stopped seeking warm meals, so he began serving family-style food from his own dining table at the back of the station. His signature dish was pan-fried chicken seasoned with a secret recipe of 11 herbs and spices. His culinary reputation grew so rapidly that Governor Ruby Laffoon commissioned him as an honorary Kentucky Colonel in 1935 in recognition of his contribution to state cuisine.

To reduce cooking time without sacrificing moisture and tenderness, Colonel Sanders revolutionized fast food preparation by adapting commercial pressure cookers into commercial pressure fryers. In 1952, at the age of sixty-two, Sanders franchised his "Kentucky Fried Chicken" concept to Pete Harman of Salt Lake City, Utah, creating the iconic bucket packaging and trademark recipe. Dressed in his trademark pristine white suit, black string tie, and sporting a white goatee, Colonel Sanders became an internationally recognized corporate ambassador and symbol of American perseverance. Today, Kentucky Fried Chicken operates tens of thousands of restaurants worldwide.
