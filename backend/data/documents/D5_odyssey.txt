Title: Odyssey by Homer - Epic Poem of Odysseus and the Journey Home
Subject: Ancient Greek Epic Poetry and Classical Mythology
Key Characters: Odysseus, Penelope, Telemachus, Athena, Polyphemus, Circe

The "Odyssey" is one of two major ancient Greek epic poems attributed to Homer, alongside its companion work the "Iliad". Composed in dactylic hexameter near the end of the eighth century BCE, the Odyssey is a foundational masterpiece of Western literature that recounts the ten-year voyage of Odysseus, King of Ithaca, as he strives to return home following the legendary siege and fall of Troy.

While Odysseus endures countless perils at sea, his loyal wife Penelope and their son Telemachus must contend with a ruthless band of suitors who have overrun the royal palace in Ithaca, consuming Odysseus's wealth and demanding Penelope's hand in marriage. Aided by the wisdom of the goddess Athena, young Telemachus embarks on a journey to Pylos and Sparta in search of news of his long-lost father.

Meanwhile, Odysseus faces trials that test his courage, wit, and endurance:
- The Cyclops Polyphemus: Trapped in a cave by the giant one-eyed son of Poseidon, Odysseus conceals his identity by calling himself "Nobody" (Outis), blinds the beast with a heated wooden stake, and escapes under the bellies of sheep.
- The Sorceress Circe: The enchantress who turns Odysseus's crew into swine on the island of Aeaea, but is subdued with the magic herb moly provided by Hermes.
- The Underworld (Nekuia): Journeying to the realm of Hades, Odysseus consults the blind prophet Tiresias, who foretells his destiny, and meets the spirits of fallen comrades including Agamemnon and Achilles.
- The Sirens, Scylla, and Charybdis: Navigating past the alluring, deadly songs of the Sirens by tying himself to the ship's mast, followed by a perilous passage between the multi-headed sea monster Scylla and the treacherous whirlpool Charybdis.
- The Island of Calypso: Seven years held captive on the island of Ogygia until Zeus orders her to release him.

Upon finally reaching Ithaca disguised as a ragged beggar, Odysseus reunites with Telemachus and Penelope's faithful nurse Eurycleia. During Penelope's archery contest using Odysseus's great bow, only Odysseus can string the mighty bow and shoot an arrow through twelve axe-heads. Reclaiming his kingdom and slaying the insolent suitors, Odysseus restores peace, honor, and justice to Ithaca.
