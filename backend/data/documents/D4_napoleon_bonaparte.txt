Title: Napoleon Bonaparte - Emperor of the French and Military Strategist
Subject: European History, Napoleonic Wars, and Legal Reform
Key Figures: Napoleon Bonaparte, Arthur Wellesley (Duke of Wellington)

Napoleon Bonaparte (1769–1821), born Napoleone di Buonaparte in Ajaccio, Corsica, was a French military commander and political leader who rose to prominence during the French Revolution and went on to conquer and dominate continental Europe as Emperor of the French from 1804 to 1814, and again briefly in 1815. Widely recognized as one of the greatest military geniuses in human history, Napoleon revolutionized military tactics, organization, training, and artillery deployment.

Following his education at the École Militaire in Paris, young Napoleon demonstrated remarkable strategic mastery at the Siege of Toulon in 1793. His brilliant Italian Campaigns of 1796 to 1797 catapulted him to national hero status. In November 1799, Napoleon seized political power in the Coup of 18 Brumaire, establishing the French Consulate and appointing himself First Consul. In December 1804, he famously crowned himself Emperor of the French at Notre-Dame Cathedral in Paris.

Napoleon's battlefield prowess shone through historic victories such as the Battle of Austerlitz in 1805 (often considered his tactical masterpiece), the Battle of Jena-Auerstedt, and the Battle of Friedland, which dissolved the Holy Roman Empire and brought most of continental Europe under his direct or allied sphere. Beyond warfare, Napoleon left an enduring administrative and civil legacy through the Napoleonic Code (Code Civil des Français), which codified equality before the law, secular legal jurisdiction, and property rights across modern Europe and Latin America.

However, the disastrous French invasion of Russia in 1812 marked a fatal turning point. The Coalition forces invaded France and forced Napoleon to abdicate in 1814, exiling him to the Mediterranean island of Elba. Escaping Elba during the "Hundred Days", Napoleon reclaimed power before his decisive defeat at the Battle of Waterloo in June 1815 by British and Prussian forces led by the Duke of Wellington. Napoleon spent his remaining years in exile on the remote South Atlantic island of Saint Helena until his death in 1821.
